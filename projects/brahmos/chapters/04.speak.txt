[a documentary narrator explaining to a friend, warm and clear, steady pace] The answer is the Ramjet Engine, one of the simplest engines ever built, because it has no moving parts at all.
A normal jet engine uses fans and a compressor to squeeze the air, and a turbine at the back to spin them.
A ramjet does the squeezing with speed alone, kind of like the wind that pushes against your face on a fast motorbike.
The air hits the Intake Cone in the nose and forms shock waves, and every shock squeezes the air tighter and heats it up.
Liquid fuel is sprayed into that hot, squeezed air, it burns and expands, and it blasts out of the Nozzle to push the missile forward.
[pause]
There's a catch, though. The air only gets squeezed if the missile is already moving fast, so a ramjet sitting still produces no thrust at all.
That's why the booster pushes it past the speed of sound first, and the ramjet then holds it at nearly Mach two point eight.
