| Booster burn time | about 4 to 6 s | https://anantamias.com/brahmos-missile/ (exam-prep site, no citation) | **low**. No official figure is published. Narrate it as "a few seconds". |
| A ramjet has no rotating compressor. The missile's forward speed rams air through the inlet, where shocks compress it. **Modelling notes (proportions and colours, from the photos and CSIS):**
- Body: a long cylinder with a length-to-diameter ratio of about 12 to 14 (8.4 m by 0.6 to 0.7 m). Display and parade missiles are mostly white or very light grey. Some have a darker grey or black nose ring, and Indian-tricolour or "BRAHMOS" lettering on the side.
- Nose: an open annular **air intake** at the very front, with a **pointed central cone (spike)** inside it. The cone tip sticks out slightly ahead of the intake lip. The intake lip is about the full body diameter.
- Wings: **4 clipped-delta wings** at mid-body in an X or + layout. They are small, with a span only about 2 to 3 times the body diameter, and fold flat for the canister.
- Tail: **4 small delta control fins** near the rear, in line with the wings.
- Booster: a separate cylinder of the same diameter at the tail with its own small fins or nozzle cover. In the integral layout it sits inside the rear (combustion chamber) section. Treat it as an illustrative separable tail section about 1.5 to 2 m long **[EST]**.
- Canister (TLC): a plain olive-green or grey tube slightly longer and wider than the missile, with end caps. The MAL carries 3 canisters side by side, horizontal for travel and raised vertical to fire. The truck is army green or desert camouflage, with a 12x12 chassis and a box cab.
- BrahMos-NG: about 6 m by 0.5 m. It looks like a slimmer, shorter copy of BrahMos.
